Today. An SD card or USB drive is a plain storage card named by its volume ID (1A2B-3C4D), with nothing saying it can be pulled out. A folder from another app (Termux's home, a Google Drive folder) has no size to read, so it shows "—" and "Size unknown - may not be readable", which looks broken while it works.

SD card and USB drive. Their own icons, named by what the system calls them (the card's brand, the drive's label). An eject mark opens Android's safe removal, because only the system can unmount. While Filet writes to one, the caption turns amber and says don't unplug. A drive in a format the phone can't read (often NTFS) says so instead of vanishing.

Older phones. On Android 8 to 10 an app can't write to an SD card until it's allowed once. The card says Read only, and Allow writing opens the system picker on that card. After that, copy, move and delete work there like on the phone's own storage.

The registry. Every SD card and USB drive is remembered by its volume ID. Pull it out and its card goes; one line says what isn't plugged in, and opens a list with last seen, Search its files (the index is kept) and Forget. Plug it back in and it's the same drive: its name, its index, its place on Home.

Folders from apps. The app's own icon and name in the heading, the folder's name as the big line, and what can be known underneath: the item count and whether Filet can write there. No capacity bar, since there is no capacity; a dotted line keeps the row even. Holding it offers Open in the app, and Remove access, which hands the permission back.

Two choices to make here: one row of cards or grouped by kind; and for several folders from the same app, one card each or one per app.
